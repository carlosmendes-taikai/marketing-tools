Most people read the headline and nothing else. That's not a reason to despair; it's a reason to spend real time on the headline. A great article with a dull title gets ignored, while a clear, intriguing title earns the click, the share and the ranking.

## What makes a headline work

Good headlines tend to do a few things well. Not all of them at once, but at least two or three:

1. **Clarity.** The reader instantly knows what it's about. Clever wordplay that needs decoding loses to plain words.
2. **Specificity.** Numbers, time frames, named audiences and concrete words: "for small teams", "in 30 days", "AI slop".
3. **A benefit.** What the reader gets: grow, save, avoid, learn, fix.
4. **A hook.** A reason to click now: curiosity, a mistake, a contrast, a bold opinion, a surprising result.
5. **The right length for the place.** Google shows about 60 characters; inboxes about 40; Google Ads allows 30.

## Headline formulas that work

### For blog posts and SEO

- **How to [result] in [time frame] (without [pain])**: How to Grow Your Newsletter in 30 Days (Without Paid Ads)
- **[Number] proven ways to [result]**: 11 Proven Ways to Cut Cart Abandonment
- **[Topic]: the complete guide for [audience]**: Email Deliverability: The Complete Guide for Startups
- **Why [belief] is wrong (and what to do instead)**: Why Posting Daily Is Wrong (and What to Do Instead)
- **[A] vs [B]: which is better for [audience]?**: Notion vs Coda: Which Is Better for Small Teams?

### For LinkedIn and X hooks

- **I [did something surprising]. [Result].**: I deleted 40% of our blog posts. Traffic went up.
- **Most [people] get [topic] wrong. Here's why.**
- **Unpopular opinion: [contrarian take].**
- **[Number] years in [field]. [Number] lessons I wish I knew sooner.**

### For ads

- **[Verb] [result] in [time frame]**: Get Payroll Done in 5 Minutes
- **Save [number]% on [product]**
- **Start your free [length] trial**

## Common headline mistakes

- **Being vague.** "Some thoughts on hiring" tells nobody why to click. Name the actual thing.
- **Jargon.** "Leveraging synergy for innovative solutions" says nothing. Plain words win.
- **Overused phrases.** "You'll love", "game-changer", "must-read" have been read a million times.
- **Clickbait.** "You won't believe…" gets ignored by readers and buried by feeds.
- **Burying the keyword.** For SEO, put the main search phrase near the start.

## Frequently asked questions

### What makes a good headline?

Clarity first, then at least one or two of: a specific detail, a clear benefit and a reason to click now. Keep it to the right length for where it's shown.

### How long should a blog post title be?

About 50 to 60 characters, so Google shows it in full, with the main keyword near the start.

### Do numbers in headlines really work?

Specific numbers make headlines feel concrete and scannable, and list-style headlines consistently perform well. Use them when they're honest and meaningful.
