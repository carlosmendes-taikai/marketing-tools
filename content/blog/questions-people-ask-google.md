The best content answers a question someone already has. And people tell Google their questions all day long: "how do I…", "why is my…", "can you…". If you know those questions, you know exactly what to write, what to put in your FAQ, and what objections your sales page needs to handle.

## Why questions are so valuable

- **They show intent.** "What is a UTM parameter" is someone learning. "UTM parameters for Facebook ads" is someone doing. You can match each with the right content.
- **They're easier to rank for.** Question searches are specific, and many have weak answers on page one.
- **They win featured snippets.** Google often shows a direct answer box for questions. A clear, short answer near the top of your page can earn that spot.
- **They reveal objections.** "Is X worth it", "X vs Y", "does X work without Y" are exactly the doubts that stop people buying.

## Method 1: Google autocomplete with question words

Type a question word followed by your topic, like "why email signature" or "how QR code", and Google completes it with real searches. The question words worth trying are how, what, why, where, when, who, which, can, does, is, are, should and will.

## Method 2: "People also ask"

Search your topic on Google and look for the **People also ask** box. Each time you open a question, Google adds more below it. Those are questions real searchers ask after your topic, and they make great section headings and FAQ items.

## Method 3: Forums and communities

Reddit, Quora, niche forums and Facebook groups are full of questions phrased in your audience's own words. Search "your topic site:reddit.com" on Google to find threads. Pay attention to the questions that get many replies or upvotes.

## Method 4: Your own inbox

Your sales calls, support tickets and DMs are the most underrated question source. If three customers asked it this month, hundreds of people searched it.

## What to do with the questions

**Write one answer-first article per big question.** Put a direct two or three sentence answer at the top, then expand. That format suits both readers and featured snippets.

**Build an FAQ section.** Group the smaller questions at the end of a related article or product page. Marking them up as FAQ structured data can help them appear in search results, which is what every guide on this site does.

**Fix your landing pages.** If people keep asking "does it work without X", say so on your homepage.

**Feed your social content.** Each question is a ready-made LinkedIn post or short video. Our [LinkedIn post formatter](/linkedin) helps you shape it before posting.

### How do I get my answer into Google's featured snippet?
