Your subject line has one job: get the email opened. Everything else (the design, the copy, the offer) only matters if that happens. The good news is that strong subject lines follow a handful of patterns you can reuse.

## What makes a subject line work

- **It's short.** Phones show about 30 to 40 characters. Aim for 4 to 9 words, with the important part first.
- **It's specific.** "3 ways to win back lost customers" beats "Tips for your business".
- **It creates a reason to open now:** curiosity, a benefit, urgency or relevance.
- **It sounds like a person,** not a brand shouting.
- **It matches the email.** Clickbait gets opens once, then unsubscribes.

## Subject line formulas with examples

### 1. The specific number

**Formula:** [Number] ways to [result] [time frame]
- 3 ways to win back lost customers this week
- 5 cold email templates that got replies
- 7 mistakes killing your open rates

### 2. The personal question

**Formula:** [Name], quick question about [topic]
- {first_name}, quick question about your launch
- Are you still hiring designers?
- Did you see this?

### 3. The mistake or cost

**Formula:** The [topic] mistake costing you [result]
- The pricing mistake costing you sales
- You're probably making this UTM mistake
- Why your emails land in spam

### 4. The update that matters

**Formula:** Your [thing] is [changing]
- Your free trial ends tomorrow
- Your order is on its way
- Your spot is saved for Thursday

### 5. The curiosity line

**Formula:** a short, honest teaser
- We almost didn't send this
- This changed how we write emails
- We messed up

### 6. The direct benefit

**Formula:** [Get result] without [pain]
- Book more calls without cold calling
- Grow your list without paid ads

## Cold email subject lines

Cold emails work best when they look like an email from a colleague, not a campaign: short, lowercase-friendly and specific.

- quick idea for {company}
- {mutual_connection} suggested I reach out
- question about {their recent post or launch}
- 15 minutes next week?

Avoid fake "Re:" or "Fwd:" tricks. They get opens and destroy trust.

## Words and habits that hurt deliverability

Spam filters and humans both react badly to:

- **Spam trigger phrases:** "act now", "guaranteed", "100% free", "click here", "risk-free".
- **ALL CAPS and lots of exclamation marks.**
- **Vague newsletter labels** like "October newsletter" or "Issue #12". Name the best thing inside instead.
- **Misleading subjects** that don't match the content.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should an email subject line be?

Around 30 to 45 characters, or 4 to 9 words. Phones show about 40 characters, so put the important part first.

### What words should you avoid in email subject lines?

Spam-trigger phrases like "act now", "guaranteed", "click here" and "100% free", plus ALL CAPS and multiple exclamation marks.

### Should I use emojis in subject lines?

Sparingly. One relevant emoji can stand out in a busy inbox, but it can look unprofessional in B2B or cold emails. Test it with your audience.

### How do I test an email subject line?

Score it in the mktbox headline analyzer with the Email subject setting, then A/B test your best two versions with a small part of your list before sending to everyone.
