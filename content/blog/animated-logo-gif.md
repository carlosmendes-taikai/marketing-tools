A logo that moves a little gets noticed. A logo that spins, flashes and bounces gets ignored, or worse, looks cheap. The trick with animated logos is restraint: one small movement, then stillness.

Here's how to make one that looks like a designer did it, without a designer.

## Where animated logos work well

- **Email signatures.** A small shine or float catches the eye in a sea of plain text. GIFs are the one animation format Gmail, Outlook and Apple Mail all show.
- **Website headers and loading screens.** A gentle fade in or wipe makes a first impression feel finished.
- **Social posts and profiles.** An animated logo in a post stands out as people scroll, as long as it doesn't shout.
- **Presentations and newsletters.** A logo that settles into place makes an intro or closing slide feel polished.

## Which animations look professional

Subtle effects age well. These are the ones brands actually use:

1. **Shine.** A soft light sweeps across the logo, then it rests. Classy, and great for premium or product brands.
2. **Float.** The logo bobs a few pixels up and down. Calm and friendly, perfect for signatures.
3. **Pulse.** A slow, small breathing in and out. Good for icons and app marks.
4. **Fade in.** The logo rises softly into view, holds, then repeats. Works for intros.
5. **Wipe.** The logo is revealed from left to right. Strong for wordmarks.
6. **Drop in.** The logo lands with one small bounce. Playful, without being silly.

What to avoid: constant spinning, flashing, rainbow color cycling and anything that never stops moving. If the animation is the first thing people notice, it's too much.

## Tips for a crisp result

- **Start big.** Upload the largest, sharpest version of your logo you have. It's scaled down cleanly; small images can't be scaled up.
- **Keep it small for email.** Aim for under 300 KB in signatures. A smaller width and a shorter loop both help.
- **Mind the colors.** GIFs use up to 256 colors. Flat logos look perfect. Photos and heavy gradients can show slight banding.
- **Transparent or solid?** Transparent GIFs have hard edges, so choose a solid background matching where the logo will sit if you want perfectly smooth edges.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I make my logo into a GIF?

Upload your logo to a GIF animator, pick an animation like shine, float or pulse, adjust the speed and size, and download the GIF. The free mktbox GIF animator does it in your browser, without an account.

### What size should an animated logo GIF be?

For email signatures, 200 to 400 pixels wide and under 300 KB. For websites, match the width it will display at, ideally at twice that size for sharp screens.

### Can an animated GIF have a transparent background?

Yes, but GIF transparency is all or nothing, with no soft edges. Tell the tool what color the logo will sit on, so its edges blend into that background.

### Do animated GIFs work in Outlook?

Yes, most current versions of Outlook play GIFs. Some older Outlook desktop versions only show the first frame, so make sure the first frame looks good on its own.
