A small animated banner under your name is one of the few ways to make an email stand out without being annoying. Think of a looping product shot, a "We're hiring" banner or a subtle logo animation. Done right, it gets clicks. Done wrong, it's a broken image icon or a 4 MB file that makes your emails crawl.

## Do GIFs work in email signatures?

Mostly, yes:

| Email app | Animated GIFs |
| --- | --- |
| Gmail (web and mobile) | Play normally |
| Apple Mail (Mac, iPhone, iPad) | Play normally |
| Outlook on the web and Outlook mobile | Play normally |
| Outlook for Windows | Newer versions play them; some older versions show only the first frame |

Because some Outlook versions freeze on the first frame, **make sure the first frame works on its own.** Put your key message or logo in frame one, not halfway through the loop.

## The one rule: the GIF must be hosted online

Email signatures can't carry images inside them. Gmail and Outlook strip out embedded images, so every image in a signature has to live at a public web address, and the signature simply points to it.

That's why "drag a GIF into the signature box" often works for you and shows a broken icon for everyone else. The file only existed on your computer.

The easy way around this is a signature tool that hosts the image for you.

## How to make a good signature GIF

- **Keep it under 1 MB.** Heavy images load slowly, look spammy to filters and eat your recipients' mobile data. Fewer frames and fewer colours shrink a GIF dramatically.
- **Size it for the inbox.** Around 400 to 600 pixels wide is plenty. Export it at that size rather than shrinking a huge file.
- **Slow and subtle beats flashy.** A gentle 3 to 5 second loop feels premium. Fast flashing feels like a 2008 banner ad.
- **Make frame one count,** for the Outlook versions that freeze there.
- **Add a clear destination.** A GIF that links somewhere useful turns attention into clicks.

You can turn a short screen recording or video into a GIF with free tools like Giphy's GIF maker or Ezgif, then trim it under 1 MB.

## Common problems and fixes

**The GIF shows as a broken image.** The file isn't publicly hosted. Re-upload it through the signature generator, or use a public link.

**The GIF doesn't move.** You're probably viewing it in an Outlook version that only shows the first frame. Check in Gmail or Apple Mail.

**Emails started going to spam.** Large images and image-heavy signatures can hurt deliverability. Keep the GIF small and make sure there's real text in your signature too.

### Can you put a GIF in an email signature?

Yes. Gmail, Apple Mail and Outlook on the web and mobile play animated GIFs. The GIF must be hosted at a public web address, which the mktbox signature generator does for you.
