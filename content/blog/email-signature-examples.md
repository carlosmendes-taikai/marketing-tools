Your email signature is the business card people see in every single email you send. Most are either bare ("Sent from my iPhone") or overloaded with six logos, a quote and a legal essay. The good ones sit in between: clear, scannable and useful.

Below are the elements every signature needs, the ones to skip, and examples for different roles you can adapt in a couple of minutes.

## What a professional email signature includes

Keep to what helps someone reach you or understand who you are:

1. **Full name.** The one people know you by.
2. **Job title and company.** One line: "Head of Growth · Bakery Co".
3. **One or two ways to reach you.** Phone and website are usually enough, since they already have your email.
4. **A photo or logo.** Optional, but a friendly photo makes cold emails feel warmer. Use a logo for shared or team addresses.
5. **Social links.** Only the ones you actually use for work, usually one to three.
6. **One call to action.** "Book a call", "See our latest work", "Read the case study". One, not five.

## What to leave out

- **Your email address.** They're replying to it.
- **Inspirational quotes.** They age badly and distract from your name.
- **Huge images.** Anything over about 1 MB loads slowly and can push emails toward spam.
- **Every social network you've ever joined.** Pick the ones that support your work.
- **Long legal disclaimers,** unless your industry requires them. If it does, keep them small and at the very bottom.

## Email signature examples

### Founder or executive

> **Ana Silva**
> Founder & CEO · Bakery Co
> P +351 912 345 678 · W bakery.co
> LinkedIn · X
> [Book a 15-minute intro]

A short, confident signature with one clear next step.

### Marketer

> **Ana Silva**
> Head of Growth · Bakery Co
> W bakery.co
> LinkedIn · Instagram
> [Animated banner: "Our spring menu is live"]

Marketers can use the signature as a small ad slot. An animated banner promoting the current campaign gets noticed without being pushy. See [how to add a GIF to your email signature](/blog/gif-email-signature).

### Freelancer or consultant

> **Ana Silva**
> Brand designer
> W anasilva.design · Portfolio on Behance and Dribbble
> [See recent work]

Lead with what you do and where to see proof.

### Student or job seeker

> **Ana Silva**
> Marketing student · University of Lisbon, class of 2027
> LinkedIn · Portfolio
> anasilva.com

Keep it simple and point to the one place that shows your work.

### Shared team inbox

> **The Bakery Co team**
> Orders and support · Mon to Fri, 9:00 to 18:00
> W bakery.co/help · P +351 912 345 678

Use the company logo instead of a person's photo, and say when people can expect a reply.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should a professional email signature include?

Your full name, job title and company, one or two contact details such as phone and website, and optionally a photo, a few social links and one call to action.

### Should I put a photo in my email signature?

A photo helps in sales, recruiting and any role where people email you for the first time. Use a clear, friendly headshot, kept small. For shared or support inboxes, use a logo instead.

### How long should an email signature be?

Aim for three to five short lines plus icons. If it's taller than the email itself, it's too long.
